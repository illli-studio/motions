# Sources and scientific boundaries
Checked 2026-10-27.

1. NASA Glenn Research Center, Torque (Moment): https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/torque-moment/ — torque depends on force and perpendicular distance to the pivot; radial force yields zero torque.
2. University of Guelph Department of Physics: https://www.physics.uoguelph.ca/torque-and-rotational-motion-tutorial — torque rF sin(theta), tangential component, distinction from energy.
3. OpenStax, University Physics Vol. 1, Work and Power for Rotational Motion: https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/10-8-work-and-power-for-rotational-motion — work for a torque integrated over angular displacement.

Our illustrative values (not measurements): with perpendicular force, 0.20 m × 100 N = 0.40 m × 50 N = 20 N·m. Equal 30° turns give arc lengths 0.10472/0.20944 m and equal ideal work 10.47198 J. Forces always tangential during this controlled example.

Opening scene is a separate qualitative same-force comparison against a resistance threshold, not a claim that equal torque gives different motion. Later equal-torque demonstration compares ideal work over equal angular displacement and ignores tool deformation/losses. Wrench length means pivot-to-force-point distance, not overall tool silhouette. Not a repair instruction; do not infer safe working torques from this schematic. All visual/temporal scales simplified.
