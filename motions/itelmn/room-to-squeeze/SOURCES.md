# Scientific sources (accessed 2026-10-27)
- Arizona State University Physics Instructional Resource Team: https://pirt.asu.edu/demos/2B20.71 . Same-weight syringe demonstration: air volume decreases visibly whereas water changes negligibly; molecules in gas are more widely separated.
- Institute of Physics: https://spark.iop.org/feeling-springiness-air . Sealed air syringe resists compression and springs back when force is reduced. Residual bubbles in a water syringe would compress.
- US Geological Survey: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/water-compressibility . Water is nearly, not absolutely, incompressible under ordinary conditions; sufficient pressure does reduce its volume.

## Accuracy boundaries
Identical illustrated piston areas and equal qualitative external force; no force or volume measurement claimed. Particle size, counts, spacing, motion and apparatus are schematic, not to scale. Particles do not shrink. Gas and liquid panels are not equal-mole samples. Liquid particles retain small spaces and motion, not a solid lattice model. Water syringe is full without a gas pocket. Tiny water compression is not measurable from artwork. Air rebound assumes a movable low-friction plunger; real friction changes response. No needles, procedural safety instructions or industrial pressure claims.
