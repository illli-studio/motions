# Scientific sources
Checked 2026-10-27. This is a simplified RGB-stripe illustration, not a measured display or a claim that all screens share this structure.
- Exploratorium, What is Color? https://annex.exploratorium.edu/colorfest/whatiscolor.html — red and green light may be perceived as yellow; perceived colors can match despite differing spectra.
- Samsung Display, display glossary: https://global.samsungdisplay.com/31427?category=57&page=1&type=list — RGB display subpixels. Search-indexed source; direct fetch timed out during research, not represented as directly retrieved.
- MIT Design FTW, Color: https://designftw.mit.edu/lectures/color/ — subpixel arrangements vary.
- HKUST institutional repository, thesis section 4.1.1: https://repository.hkust.edu.hk/ir/bitstream/1783.1-87531/1/b1731778.pdf — spatial color integration and subpixel structure.
- Sharp official RGBY announcement: https://global.sharp/corporate/news/100531_1.html — yellow subpixels exist in certain display designs, hence the scope limitation.
## Wording boundaries
The yellow panel is an illustration of appearance, not a spectral simulation. The two spectral charts are qualitative schematic bands, not measured/numerical spectra and not claims of precise metameric matching. A mixture does not create a new yellow wavelength. No paint mixing comparison, biological cone simplification, or all-color gamut claim is made.
