# Scientific sources — Steel Can Float
Verified 2026-10-27. All animation is schematic, not a measurement or a scale model.

1. NASA Glenn, “Buoyancy: Archimedes Principle”. https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/WindTunnel/Activities/buoy_Archimedes.html
   Supports: an immersed object experiences upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces. Force is not mass. A fully submerged dense solid still has buoyancy, but it can be less than its weight.
2. NOAA Office of Education, “Build a boat”. https://www.noaa.gov/office-education/outreach-communication/hands-on-science-activities/build-a-boat
   Supports: boat shape, size and height affect displaced-water volume and loading capacity; water entering over low sides can cause sinking; balance/stability matters separately.
3. Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum, “Ships and Bouyancy”. https://howthingsfly.si.edu/media/ships-and-bouyancy
   Access note: search-index excerpt read; direct page opening returned an OCIO rejection, so this is supplementary rather than the sole basis. Supports: a loaded floating ship sits lower and displaces more water; in floating equilibrium, its weight equals displaced-water weight.
4. Transport Canada, Fourth-class Engineer study guide, section 3.2.2, Archimedes’ principle. https://tc.canada.ca/en/marine-transportation/publications/tp-14609-study-guide-applicants-fourth-class-engineer-certificate-stcw-endorsement
   Supports: freely floating at rest displaces a mass of water equal to its own mass. A gradually immersed vessel can have buoyancy less than weight while an external support supplies the balance.

## Wording used / boundaries
- Steel does not become lighter when reshaped. Same mass is a controlled comparison, not a claim that every depicted boat and cube is to scale.
- A broad hollow hull excludes water from more volume before its rim is submerged; it can achieve buoyancy equal to total weight while still keeping water out.
- Static afloat: buoyancy equals total weight. Sinking: buoyancy can be less than weight; a submerged steel object is not without buoyancy.
- Added load causes deeper draft and larger displacement at the new floating equilibrium, within safe capacity.
- Excess load or flooding can remove the possibility of a stable floating equilibrium with the rim above water. Flooding is not a claim that every hole makes every vessel instantly sink.
- No numeric steel density or measured force data are claimed on screen. Geometry uses a representative steel-to-water density ratio of 7.8 solely for internally consistent draught; real alloys and water density vary. Surface tension, waves and detailed stability are omitted.
