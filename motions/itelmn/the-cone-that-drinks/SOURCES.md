# Scientific sources (checked 2026-10-28)
1. Reyssat E, Mahadevan L. Hygromorphs: from pine cones to biomimetic bilayers. Journal of the Royal Society Interface (2009). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2838359/ DOI10.1098/rsif.2009.0184. Supports passive humidity-driven motion of dead fallen cone scales, differential longitudinal swelling and concentrated basal bending.
2. Ulrich et al. The Structural and Mechanical Basis for Passive-Hydraulic Pine Cone Actuation (2022). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9284161/ . Supports abaxial sclereid/adaxial fiber tissue differential behavior. Fiber layer also absorbs water and changes mechanics: it is not inert.
3. Orchestrated Movement Sequences and Shape-Memory-like Effects in Pine Cones (2024). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11313876/ . Supports reversible wet/dry closing/opening and tissue complexity. No inference that every species or initially resin-sealed cone behaves identically.
## Scope
This shows a mature, previously open cone. It is not the initial opening of a resin-sealed serotinous cone. Geometric proportions, colour-coded layers and movement timing are explanatory simplifications. Water uptake is distributed; droplets are a wet-environment cue, not a claim that one drop instantly triggers closure. Actual motion may take minutes/hours depending on tissue, water availability and conditions; this film has no real-time scale.
