# Condensation science: independent source review

Checked 2026-10-27. All visuals are original explanatory diagrams, not experimental footage or measurement. No source illustrations or text are copied.

## Primary sources

1. NOAA JetStream, **Learning Lesson: Sweatin’ to the Coldies**
   https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/ll-sweatin
   Opened successfully. Its classroom cold-glass demonstration explicitly identifies atmospheric water as the source of external droplets and cooling near the glass below the dew point as the mechanism. This supports the core question/answer, not a measured 27-second time-to-condense.
2. NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory, **Water Vapor Instrument Description — The Basic Principle**
   https://gml.noaa.gov/ozwv/wvap/instrument.html
   Opened successfully. Chilled-surface condensation underlies dew/frost-point hygrometry; it uses an iced-tea glass as an example. Dew point depends on water-vapor content, so no universal temperature threshold is given here.
3. NOAA/NWS La Crosse, **Dew Point vs. Humidity**
   https://www.weather.gov/arx/why_dewpoint_vs_humidity
   Opened successfully. At constant pressure, cooling air to its dew point brings it to saturation; further cooling produces net liquid condensation. Supports the variable dew-point condition rather than the misleading statement that any cooling inevitably makes droplets.
4. USGS Water Science School, **Condensation and the Water Cycle**
   https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/condensation-and-water-cycle
   Search index retrieved full relevant content, including gas-to-liquid change and cold-glass example; direct open timed out. Supplemental rather than sole evidence.

## Approved claims and boundaries

- Cup-wall droplets can come from ambient water vapor, not from water leaking through an intact impermeable glass wall. An intact closed cup is an illustrative control; the animation is not proof from an actual performed experiment.
- In humid conditions, an adequately cold surface can cool adjacent air enough for water vapor to condense on it. The clear condition for this scene is **杯壁温度低于周围空气的露点**.
- Water vapor is gaseous water, ordinarily invisible. The visible beads are liquid water. Molecule symbols must be explicitly labelled enlarged schematic, never shown as naturally visible floating blue droplets.
- Cooling changes the state of pre-existing water. It does not create water or push liquid through the glass.
- The room-temperature control should be described as **本示意条件下**; a warm/room-temperature cup is not universally guaranteed dry in every environment.
- Dew point is not always 0°C, 10°C, or any other fixed number. It varies with vapor content and pressure. Do not draw a numerical thermometer unless supported by an explicitly hypothetical coherent example.
- A single molecule is not a whole visible bead. Use many molecules accumulating at the outer surface; label particle scale/count schematic.
- Do not depict vapor or water crossing the wall, or suggest the ice creates moisture. Heat flows toward the colder cup; avoid arrows labelled cold flowing out.
- The coldest subzero surfaces can form frost; this film addresses ordinary liquid condensation on the depicted chilled glass, not every subzero regime.
- The whole process is time-compressed. Do not imply a real cup must form large drops within 27 seconds. Onscreen footnote: **过程加速；分子与水滴非等比例示意**.

## Suggested 27-second five-stage script

| Time | Main text / explanatory action | Knowledge purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 s | **冰杯外面的水，从哪儿来？** Macro external droplet begins sliding; pull back to lid and intact cup. | Question anchored in everyday observation. |
| 4–8 s | **杯子没漏，外壁也会湿。** Closed cold cup beside same intact room-temperature cup, same environment. | Distinguish the external mechanism from leakage. |
| 8–13 s | **空气里有看不见的水蒸气。** Magnification window introduces widely separated molecular symbols outside the wall. | Locate source and distinguish invisible gas. |
| 13–21 s | **杯壁低于露点 → 水蒸气凝结。** Show an actual wall cross-section separating inner water/ice from outer vapor; outside symbols gather into liquid beads without crossing glass. | Explain cause and the dew-point condition. |
| 21–27 s | **水来自空气。冷，只让它变成水滴。** Return to macro droplet and two-cup comparison; small caveat **是否凝结，取决于表面温度与空气湿度**. | Memorable accurate takeaway and boundary. |

Concise labels: **杯内 / 杯壁 / 杯外**; **水蒸气（气态） / 水滴（液态）**. Long captions should not all appear together; one main statement at a time.
