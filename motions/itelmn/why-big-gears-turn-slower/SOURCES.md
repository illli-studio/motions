# Scientific sources

Verified 2026-10-27. This is an original explanatory animation, not an engineering manufacturing drawing.

1. UC San Diego MAE 3, **Gear Ratios**: https://mae3.eng.ucsd.edu/machine-design/gear-ratios
   - Under continuous meshing (no tooth jumping), each gear passes the same number of teeth through the mesh. Rotational speed magnitudes therefore obey omega_out / omega_in = teeth_in / teeth_out.
   - The illustrated pair of external gears rotates in opposite directions. Pitch radii, rather than tip radii, define the ratio.
2. LEGO Education, **Gear**: https://education.lego.com/en-us/lessons/spm/gear/
   - Directly meshing external gears reverse direction. A small driver driving a larger follower reduces follower rotational speed.

## Scope and boundaries

The film concerns two directly meshing, fixed-axis external spur gears with equal module, no slipping or tooth jumping. The numerical example is a chosen illustration, not a measured machine. Rotational speed means turns per unit time, not the linear speed of every point on the gear. No claim of free energy, efficiency, or a torque gain is made. Tooth profiles and clearances are a visual schematic, not manufacture-ready tolerances.
